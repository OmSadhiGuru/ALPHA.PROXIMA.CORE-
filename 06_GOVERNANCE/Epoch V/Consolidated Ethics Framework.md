# Consolidated Ethics Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides one institutional entry point for the ethical commitments already distributed across the constitutional Books, the [[Ethics Council Charter]], the LUMIAION framework, and the Living Genome framework. It consolidates those commitments without replacing their source instruments or convening the Ethics Council.

## Context

CAR-001 found the Foundation's ethical principles strong but dispersed. This draft closes the documentation gap identified as M-06 by defining shared principles, review triggers, authority boundaries, and a traceable review record. Where this framework conflicts with a higher-ranked instrument, the [[Constitutional Hierarchy Statement]] controls.

## Core Content

### 1. Ethical Principles

Every Foundation action shall be evaluated against these principles:

1. **Human sovereignty** — AI augments human judgment and never silently displaces accountable human authority.
2. **Constitutional alignment** — delegated action remains within the authority, purpose, and limits of the governing instrument.
3. **Epistemic integrity** — claims carry evidence class, provenance, uncertainty, counter-evidence, and limitations appropriate to their stakes.
4. **Consent and agency** — people understand and can meaningfully accept, decline, pause, or reverse consequential uses of their data or decisions.
5. **Privacy and data stewardship** — collect the minimum necessary data; preserve ownership, portability, access control, and deletion or archival rights defined by the governing context.
6. **Non-maleficence and proportionality** — anticipated benefit must justify foreseeable harm, exposure, irreversibility, and governance burden.
7. **Fairness and dignity** — decisions must not exploit vulnerability, erase dissent, or treat a person solely as an input to an institutional objective.
8. **Transparency and explainability** — consequential recommendations and actions disclose responsible actors, reasoning, evidence, uncertainty, and machine participation.
9. **Reversibility and historical permanence** — prefer reversible action; preserve prior states and rationales when knowledge or policy changes.
10. **Accountability** — every consequential action has an identified owner, review path, and durable record.

### 2. Review Tiers

| Tier | Trigger | Required handling |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| Routine | Low-stakes, reversible, within existing delegation | Responsible actor applies this framework and records material decisions |
| Elevated | Sensitive data; health, financial, identity, or community impact; expanded AI access; external publication | Documented pre-action assessment and human approval by the accountable office |
| Mandatory Ethics Review | Class I amendment; new institutional AI authority; material automation deployment; canonisation with significant real-world implications; credible constitutional conflict | Review under the [[Ethics Council Charter]] before enactment or publication |
| Urgent Safeguard | Imminent harm or active constitutional breach | Pause or constrain the action, preserve evidence, notify the Founder and Ethics Council channel, and document the temporary measure |

The highest applicable tier governs. A system may recommend a tier but may not downgrade a human or constitutional review requirement.

### 3. Ethical Review Record

An elevated or mandatory review must record:

- the proposed action, accountable owner, and authority source;
- affected people, systems, data, and rights;
- evidence and uncertainty, including credible alternatives;
- foreseeable benefits, harms, reversibility, and failure modes;
- mitigations, residual risk, and dissent;
- decision, reviewers, date, expiry or next-review trigger; and
- links to the controlling ADR, finding, protocol, or publication record.

Absence of a named Ethics Council membership does not permit a required review to be represented as completed. Until the Council is convened, mandatory reviews remain pending or are handled only under an expressly ratified interim authority.

### 4. AI-Specific Boundaries

AI participants may investigate, compare, draft, test, and recommend within delegated scope. They must not:

- ratify Class I or Class II instruments;
- conceal uncertainty, provenance, model participation, or material limitations;
- expand their own authority, credentials, data access, or audience;
- make irreversible health, financial, legal, identity, or governance decisions for a person;
- overwrite prior knowledge states or suppress conflicting evidence; or
- claim that automated validation equals ethical or constitutional approval.

### 5. Domain Application

| Domain | Minimum ethical control |
|--------|-------------------------|
| Research and canonisation | Book III evidence class, source traceability, conflicting evidence, limitations, and required human/Ethics review |
| Software and automation | Bounded scope, least privilege, failure modes, human override, audit log, rollback path |
| AI appointments and engine changes | Role defined before engine; authority and data-access review; succession and suspension path |
| Health and personal development | Explicit consent, sensitivity controls, uncertainty, non-diagnostic boundary unless properly authorized |
| Finance and strategy | Source freshness, scenario/risk disclosure, no unapproved execution or representation of certainty |
| Publication and partnerships | Attribution, privacy, values alignment, claims review, accountable institutional approver |
| Living Genome | User ownership, portability, explainability, consent for mutation, prior-state preservation |

### 6. Governance and Interpretation

The [[Ethics Council Charter]] governs membership, investigations, findings, and veto procedure. [[Book I - The Constitution]] and [[Book III - Knowledge Integrity]] govern constitutional and epistemic obligations. This framework is a consolidated operational reference; it creates no new council, appointment, or autonomous enforcement authority.

Material conflicts are escalated through the ratified council topology: operational body → ratifying body → [[Alpha Council]] → Founder, with Ethics oversight able to enter at any level.
